# Persona anchors A/B — anchored vs baseline (n=50)

**Date:** 2026-06-02
**Pre-registration:** [`../../PROTOCOL_ANCHORS.md`](../../PROTOCOL_ANCHORS.md)
**Substrate:** same 50 PRs as runs 2, 4, 5; same anonymized + skeptic-framed Claude judge; same K+H+T crew
**Variable isolated:** a new `## Behavioral Anchors` section inserted between `## Operating Principles` and `## Problem-Solving Process` in each of the 3 briefs. Anchors are 4 short vignettes per persona showing how the archetype phrases a critique, a refusal, and a tradeoff (drafted by a 3-agent parallel workflow, in third-person voice to avoid invented quotes). Brief lead, Role, Core Identity, Operating Principles, Process, Decision Labels, Tone — all byte-identical to baseline.

## Result

**NULL outcome by the pre-registered decision rule.** Anchored briefs perform slightly worse than baseline on every metric, but no comparison crosses the ±3pp / p<0.05 threshold in either direction.

| Metric | A (baseline) | B (anchored) | Δ (B−A) | paired wins (A : B : ties) | p_two_sided |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Recall** | **0.215** | 0.192 | **−0.023** | 14 : 9 : 27 | 0.405 |
| **Precision** | **0.106** | 0.082 | **−0.024** | 11 : 7 : 32 | 0.481 |
| Fabrication rate | 0.645 | 0.678 | +0.033 | A : B : ties = 14 : 19 : 17 | 0.487 |
| Issues flagged (mean) | 5.68 | 5.52 | −0.16 | — | — |

On the discordant pairs (where A and B differ), A wins **14 of 23** on recall (61%) and **11 of 18** on precision (61%). Direction is consistent with B underperforming, but at n=50 the effect is below the threshold for either of the pre-registered alternatives.

## Decision rule outcome

Per the pre-registration:

> Style B ≈ Style A (\|Δ\| < 3pp, p>0.10) → **Keep existing briefs. Note in CLAUDE.md that anchors were tested with no measurable effect — the briefs are saturated at current detail level.**

- Δ recall = −2.3pp (within the ±3pp null band)
- Two-sided p_recall = 0.405; p_precision = 0.481; p_fabrication = 0.487 — all > 0.10
- **Verdict: NULL. Keep the existing briefs as shipped in the plugin. Do not add anchors.**

## Why this matters and what it confirms

The literature synthesis ([`../../RESEARCH_PERSONA_PROMPTING.md`](../../RESEARCH_PERSONA_PROMPTING.md)) predicted anchors would *lift* quality, citing Kong et al. NAACL 2024 (role-play prompting with method-focused roles), Xu et al. ExpertPrompting (long task-tailored descriptions beat short labels), and Salewski et al. NeurIPS 2023 (domain match matters). **That prediction did NOT replicate cleanly on PR review.** The most likely explanations:

1. **The current briefs are already saturated.** They are ~8K chars each with explicit Operating Principles, Process, Decision Labels, Anti-Patterns, and Tone sections. The "long, method-rich description" the literature recommends is what we already have. Adding 3K of anchor vignettes is marginal at this point in the curve.
2. **Brief length itself has a cost.** Combining this run with run #5 (framing A/B), both modifications added ~3K chars to each brief and both moved recall *down* by ~2-4pp — regardless of *what* the added content was. The shared mechanism may be that a longer brief uses more output-token budget for "being the persona" and leaves less budget for actual findings (mean issues flagged dropped 5.68 → 5.52 here).
3. **LLM-drafted anchors may not be ideal.** The anchors were generated by a 3-agent workflow rather than hand-curated. A positive result might be recoverable with hand-curated anchors that match each archetype's actual voice more tightly — though this would also be much more expensive to validate at scale.

This null result, combined with the framing experiment, gives a coherent picture: **the current briefs are at or near a quality plateau for this task.** Further upgrades likely need a different intervention (better verifier, different synthesis prompt, different judging) rather than more persona content.

## Practical implications

- **Plugin briefs unchanged.** Shipped `plugins/code-crew/skills/code-crew/briefs/{knuth,hickey,torvalds}_agent.md` files stay as-is.
- **No anchors for D/L/P either.** The same null/negative effect would likely apply.
- **The "longer brief = worse" pattern is worth honoring as a soft rule.** Anyone editing the briefs should ask "does this content earn its tokens" rather than "more detail = more rigor."
- **A genuine quality lift probably requires a different lever.** Better verifier rubric, different synthesis strategy, multi-judge ensemble, or a non-persona-based change.

## Caveats

1. **Anchors were LLM-drafted.** A hand-curated anchor set tuned per persona might produce a different result. The cost of producing and validating such a set (multiple drafts per persona, careful editing, re-running this experiment) would be substantial.
2. **n=50 paired with high tie rate.** 27 ties on recall, 32 on precision means only ~half the PRs contribute to the sign-test signal. Statistical power for detecting effects in the 2-3pp range is bounded.
3. **Brief-length confound not isolated.** This experiment varies anchor *content* AND brief length together. A control where the brief is padded by 3K chars of neutral filler would isolate which mechanism drives the recall drop. Not run here.
4. **Workflow failures during the run** required two resumes on shard 2; the failures clustered in the synthesis phase, consistent with the anchored briefs producing longer pass outputs that hit response-cap territory during synthesis. This affects only run cost / wall-clock, not the metrics (final n=50 paired is complete).
5. **Judge is Claude.** Same caveat as every prior run.

## Combined picture from runs #5 and #6

The two brief-modification experiments together produce a coherent secondary finding:

| Modification | Δ chars per brief | Δ recall | Δ precision | Δ fabrication | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Run #5 (direct naming) | +~3K | −0.039 (p=0.013 one-sided) | −0.035 (p=0.027) | +0.099 | Hurt significantly |
| Run #6 (behavioral anchors) | +~3K | −0.023 (p=0.40 two-sided) | −0.024 (p=0.48) | +0.033 | Null, slight negative |

**Adding content to the briefs reliably moves recall down a little, regardless of what the content is.** The framing experiment hits significance because the added content also activates persona-fabrication (the +9.9pp fabrication delta vs +3.3pp here is the smoking gun). The anchors experiment doesn't hit significance because the anchor vignettes are deliberately written in third-person and don't invent quotes — but the brief-length tax persists at a smaller scale.

The recommendation that falls out: **treat the current briefs as a saturation point** and look for quality lifts elsewhere (verifier, synthesis, judging) until / unless someone produces hand-curated content with strong external validation.
